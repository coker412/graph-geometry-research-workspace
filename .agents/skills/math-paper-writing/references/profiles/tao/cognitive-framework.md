# Problem framing and evidence strategy: the selected Tao corpus

These are explanatory choices inferred from selected texts. They are not a claim that a sampled author has one universal philosophy. All twenty are sole-authored, but T09/T10 explicitly credit earlier and collaborative ingredients; sole authorship does not erase intellectual provenance.

## L3: select the unresolved mechanism

The chosen papers often sharpen a question into a precise quantitative gap, obstruction or reformulation. T05 separates distortion from target dimension. T07 names the bottleneck of an existing packing method. T09 asks what information a possible regularity proof would have to use. T17 changes the language of a multiplier theorem to make its structure and constants clearer.

This is a useful question-selection lens, not evidence that Tao avoids other kinds of problems. The sample was selected for cross-field exposition, so the frequency of reformulations is selection-dependent. Publication timing is not evidence of an intellectual strategy.

## L4: source and explanation choices

Earlier results appear as inputs with particular jobs. T10 names the three ingredients and their provenance. T05 identifies the analogy to Nash–Moser iteration while declining to assert that a standard theorem applies verbatim. T17 supplies a concrete dictionary and norm comparison. These are better models for borrowing methods than a list of prestigious adjacent fields.

Heuristics are often made explicit, but their status varies. T01 follows a probabilistic heuristic with a formal distribution proposition. T13 deliberately leaves parts of its numerology vague in the informal overview. T16 suppresses errors only for its sketch. A writer must identify the later formal statement or openly retain the gap.

Figures can carry the whole strategy (T02), a counterexample geometry (T04), or a local construction (T07). Their captions explain use, not merely appearance. Surveys T18/T20 distinguish their audience and exposition goals from the source proof literature.

## L5: three reusable propositions

1. **A useful cross-field explanation carries a return map.** T05, PDF pp. 3–5 and later reductions; T17, pp. 1–2, 9 and Sections 2–4; T19, pp. 9–10. Name what becomes what, what estimate is gained, and how it answers the original question. Confidence: repeated across several fields in this selected corpus.
2. **Long proofs stay readable when each reduction specifies the remaining task.** T01's section sequence and p. 56, T05's Sections 4–9, T11–T12's elimination sections. Editorial action: state the new target and explain how solving it discharges the preceding one, including parameter dependence. Confidence: repeated, but not a reason to expand a short proof into an artificial chain.
3. **A limitation can be a positive mathematical contribution when its logical target is exact.** T09 p. 5, T12 p. 5, T13 pp. 3–4. A counterexample to a larger class can constrain a proof strategy without resolving the narrower original problem. Editorial action: keep those scopes separate in the abstract, overview and conclusion. Confidence: strong within the related blowup-paper family, with reduced independence because of that relationship.

These observations do not establish that adding more exposition always improves a paper. T07 shows a compact successful mechanism explanation; T18's declared omissions belong to its survey genre. No author-profile accuracy score or independent evaluation has been obtained.
