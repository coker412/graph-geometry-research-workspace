# Stanley: question, evidence and reasoning presentation (L3–L5)

[Profile entry](../../profile-stanley.md) · [Synthesis](Writing-DNA.md) · [Corpus and structures](structure-patterns.md)

**Scope:** Enumerative and algebraic combinatorics, 1996–2016. Twenty distinct complete works; 12 sole-authored and 8 collaborative. The four-author study keeps one additional prior pilot comparison per author outside these twenty. Complete files were available; selected openings, transitions, endings and structural features were read. This is not an exhaustive proof audit or an independently validated author model.

Several works share symmetric-function and tableau machinery. Coauthored articles include major contributions by others; the profile does not allocate sentences. Manuscript revision dates can differ from publication years and the author index can point to a changed version. This is not a broad sample of extremal or probabilistic combinatorics.

## Question framing (L3)

Keep counting objects, encodings and recovered statistics connected. The [individual architectures](structure-patterns.md) identify whether the gap is an unavailable estimate, an ambiguous formulation, a new invariant, a stronger identity, an obstruction or an alternative proof. Infer the article's stated problem from the text; do not infer an author's universal research agenda.

## Evidence strategy (L4)

For each borrowed passage, identify the job of the evidence: cited theorem supplies an input; construction proves existence; calculation evaluates a defined invariant; counterexample separates claims; numerical evidence suggests a question. The corpus annotations name important distinctions. A prestigious source or visually persuasive example does not replace the required implication. Collaborations and related series must be reported as such.

## Reasoning presentation (L5)

For a generating function, explain what one factor counts before extracting a coefficient. For a bijection, check that the reader can reconstruct the inverse on the stated domain.

Choose an explanatory order that follows the target proof's dependencies. Keep formal arguments, heuristic analogies and unresolved steps distinguishable at their point of use. The rules describe observable exposition, not psychological traits.

## Change from the initial small sample

The expanded sample rejects always begin with a concrete toy example. It strengthens the inverse-map lesson and the need to distinguish a proved identity from a desired alternative proof.
