# Mumford: question, evidence and reasoning presentation (L3–L5)

[Profile entry](../../profile-mumford.md) · [Synthesis](Writing-DNA.md) · [Corpus and structures](structure-patterns.md)

**Scope:** Classical algebraic geometry, 1961–1982. Twenty distinct complete works; 12 sole-authored and 8 collaborative. The four-author study keeps one additional prior pilot comparison per author outside these twenty. Complete files were available; selected openings, transitions, endings and structural features were read. This is not an exhaustive proof audit or an independently validated author model.

The sample includes related degeneration constructions and collaborations with Matsusaka, Newstead, Deligne, Harris, Oort, Horrocks, Artin and Bombieri. It is not all modern algebraic geometry or Mumford’s later work in other fields. A paper by Ness with a Mumford appendix was excluded instead of counting it as his coauthored research paper.

## Question framing (L3)

Explain why a formulation or change of category makes the original question tractable. The [individual architectures](structure-patterns.md) identify whether the gap is an unavailable estimate, an ambiguous formulation, a new invariant, a stronger identity, an obstruction or an alternative proof. Infer the article's stated problem from the text; do not infer an author's universal research agenda.

## Evidence strategy (L4)

For each borrowed passage, identify the job of the evidence: cited theorem supplies an input; construction proves existence; calculation evaluates a defined invariant; counterexample separates claims; numerical evidence suggests a question. The corpus annotations name important distinctions. A prestigious source or visually persuasive example does not replace the required implication. Collaborations and related series must be reported as such.

## Reasoning presentation (L5)

Before introducing a new category or generalized object, state the obstruction it removes. After the auxiliary theorem, translate its conclusion back to the original geometric property.

Choose an explanatory order that follows the target proof's dependencies. Keep formal arguments, heuristic analogies and unresolved steps distinguishable at their point of use. The rules describe observable exposition, not psychological traits.

## Change from the initial small sample

The larger sample adds an explicit discovery-versus-presentation example and a contrary case for repeatedly re-explaining motivation. Core instructions on useful definitions and returning from reductions already cover much of the common craft.
