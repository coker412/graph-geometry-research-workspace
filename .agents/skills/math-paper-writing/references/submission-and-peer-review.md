# Submission and peer review

Read this reference for an initial cover letter, submission package, revised manuscript, response to referees, or communication with an editor. Journal instructions and the editor's decision letter are authoritative for the particular submission and must be checked at the time of use.

Preparing files is not authorization to submit, email, upload, choose reviewers, accept a license, or pay a fee. Obtain explicit authorization immediately before any external action.

## Establish the submission contract

Identify:

- the journal, section, article type, editor when specified, and current author instructions;
- the exact manuscript title, author list, corresponding author, and source snapshot;
- whether this is an initial submission, transfer, resubmission, or requested revision;
- required declarations, enclosures, marked versions, source files, and deadlines;
- the verified status of originality, simultaneous submission, related manuscripts, preprints, conflicts, permissions, funding, data, code, and AI-use disclosures.

Do not infer or invent any declaration. In particular, never assert author consent, exclusive submission, absence of conflicts, originality, permissions, or compliance with a policy unless the researcher or an authoritative project record confirms it.

## Initial cover letter

Use the venue's form or requested structure when one exists. Otherwise keep the letter concise and professional:

1. identify the manuscript by exact title and article type;
2. state the problem, principal established result, and method at the manuscript's actual evidence level;
3. explain fit to the journal through subject, audience, or article type rather than praise;
4. include only required or genuinely decision-relevant declarations;
5. close with the corresponding author's verified contact information.

The letter must agree with the title, abstract, theorem statements, author list, bibliography, and current manuscript version. Do not call a result novel, first, complete, sharp, optimal, or a solution of an open problem unless the literature and evidence gates required by local policy are closed.

Do not suggest or exclude editors or referees unless the venue permits it and the researcher authorizes the names. Provide factual conflict information without attacking a person or speculating about motives.

## Decision letter and response ledger

Read the editor's decision before the referee reports because it determines whether a revision has been invited and which requests are mandatory. Build a ledger before drafting prose:

| ID | Source | Comment or request | Required action | Manuscript change | Location | Response | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Assign stable identifiers such as `E1`, `R1.1`, and `R2.3`. Preserve the referee's meaning; quote the comment when practical, or give a faithful labeled summary when it is very long. Separate mathematical objections, exposition requests, citation suggestions, production corrections, and optional ideas.

If reports conflict, do not silently satisfy one by ignoring another. Follow explicit editorial direction; otherwise explain the conflict and the chosen resolution, or ask the editor for clarification when the decision materially depends on it.

## Point-by-point response

Respond to every item, including points with which the authors disagree. For each item:

1. state whether the manuscript changed;
2. explain the mathematical or editorial reason;
3. identify the exact section, theorem, equation, page, line, or quoted revised passage;
4. note any downstream changes to statements, proofs, references, figures, or evidence status.

Use respectful, neutral language and answer the strongest reasonable interpretation of the comment. A disagreement should rest on a definition, proof, counterexample, computation, source, scope decision, or venue constraint. Do not dismiss a comment merely because the referee misunderstood: first check whether the manuscript caused the misunderstanding.

Never repair a mathematical objection only in the response letter. Put every correction needed for validity or reader understanding into the manuscript, then make the response describe the actual change. Conversely, do not claim a change was made unless it appears in the cited revision snapshot.

## Mathematical changes during revision

Treat a referee-triggered change as ordinary mathematical work:

- update the mathematical source of truth first;
- recheck assumptions, quantifiers, normalization, proof dependencies, citations, and evidence status;
- propagate the change to the abstract, introduction, conclusion, figures, supplements, and language versions;
- rebuild and, when local policy requires it, reverify the affected claim.

If a comment exposes a gap that cannot be closed, do not disguise it as an expository clarification. State the precise issue internally, downgrade affected claims, and obtain researcher direction before representing the result to the editor.

## Revision cover letter and package

When useful or required, accompany the detailed response with a short letter to the editor that identifies the revision, summarizes the main changes, and flags any matter requiring editorial judgment. Do not duplicate the full point-by-point response.

Before handoff, compare:

- clean and marked manuscripts;
- response ledger and response letter;
- cover letter and submission metadata;
- source files, bibliography, figures, supplements, and compiled PDFs;
- page and line references against the actual uploaded version.

Report unresolved comments, unverified declarations, missing permissions, deadline uncertainty, and any request that the authors chose not to follow. A polished response package does not imply acceptance or authorize submission.
