# Exploration and route diagnosis

Track method families by mechanism: representation, invariant, subgoal, source/exposure, falsification test, obstacle and reopening condition. Seek at least three distinct directions initially or when replanning, not every round. If the evidence supports fewer credible directions, explain that limitation and test the strongest available one; do not invent nominal alternatives to fill a quota. Pursue one main route to a complete candidate, rigorous counterexample or precise gap.

Choose routes by prospects for solving the original problem. At initial selection, before sealing a new mathematical task, or during a triggered strategy review, compare the credible routes against the full target, including parked alternatives identified in the short state/route index. Read their cited evidence as needed; do not reread whole ledgers or invent alternatives to fill a quota. A completed local task is also a decision point: expanding the same special family is not the automatic next task.

Use the scoped comparison per literature-check.md before prolonged investment: nearest known result, standard reduction tested, reusable input and remaining difference. Select work on that difference; do not reconstruct covered claims unless PLAN states a verification/concrete-extension purpose and stopping condition. Review need not search again. Inspect a relevant proof mechanism before repeated failure when it could change the route. Explicit offline-first work keeps its permitted inputs and planned comparison boundary.

For each serious option, identify the mathematical mechanism, the missing implication back to the original target, the evidence for/against it, and the next derivation or experiment whose possible outcomes would change the decision. Distinguish a method's failure from failure of the conjecture. Ask whether the obstacle belongs to the original problem or was introduced by a chosen ansatz, symmetry, discretization, bound or initial condition. Compare proof/obstruction, representation changes and imported techniques where relevant, within the search contract and information mode. An unproved bridge may itself be the best task; uncertainty does not disqualify a promising route or require a full proof architecture before exploration.

Prefer the route with the strongest supported prospect of reaching the full target and a consequential next test. A tractable subproblem earns priority when it enables that route or discriminates between mechanisms. Ease of calculation, near-threshold numerics, fact counts and accumulated effort are not enough. Do not invent numerical success probabilities or rank routes by model votes. Record the choice, decisive uncertainty and revisit condition briefly in existing notes/PLAN; update route records only for structural changes. Keep useful alternatives recoverable without running all of them each round.

Within a sealed task, concentrate on the selected gap and actually attempt the bridge. Continue a difficult route while its mechanism remains credible and the next step can resolve an outstanding obligation; lack of quick success alone does not justify switching. If evidence undermines it, preserve the failed derivation and adjust or backtrack through the existing task protocol. Never rewrite sealed acceptance or silently replace the original target with a special case.

After two failures of the same mechanism at the same obstacle, test a different representation, a weaker auxiliary statement, counterexamples or an escalation packet. A weaker auxiliary statement does not replace the original problem or sealed task acceptance. A failed attempt with a new mechanism is not automatically a repeat. Record location, scope, intermediate results, temporary/structural nature and type: gap/counterexample/circular/too-strong/definition-mismatch/reference-mismatch/missing-tool. Reopen only with a new mechanism, invariant, construction, assumption or tool attacking that gap; renaming or more agents is insufficient.

In a strategy review, perform a substantive test of the decisive missing implication, not only a comparison table. Existing evidence may already settle that test; cite it instead of rerunning it. If a computation is the next action, follow computation.md and explain what its outcomes can establish. If further samples cannot resolve the quantifier, uniform estimate, compatibility or reconstruction obligation, work on that obligation in text or choose a different mechanism. Use existing review triggers and records; add no per-call review ritual, automatic hold, new budget or model/delegation authorization.

For a persistent, precisely identified bottleneck, use agents/protocols/cross-field.md to test a structural reformulation and transfer tools back to the original problem. Reuse existing mappings; do not repeat this exercise without new evidence or a new mechanism.

Repair local errors. For failed key claims, backtrack only dependent branches, downgrade to blocked/partial-result/GAP and record why. If all routes block, continue exploration under the search contract. Main candidates require proof-audit; counterexamples also counterexample-audit.
