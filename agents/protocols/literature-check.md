# Literature checks

Only search in connected or explicitly authorized online phases. Read original papers (journal/author versions) and relevant theorem context; abstracts, snippets, authority and similar names do not establish applicability. Check assumptions, quantifiers, definitions, normalization and the proof step being used. Missing decisive text is a source GAP. Literature assessment never raises mathematical evidence levels; offline generation is not evidence of historical independence or novelty.

## Establish the known baseline before prolonged investment

In connected work, first reuse the relevant known-result records; fill missing comparisons for the original target and the substantial intermediate claim about to receive prolonged effort. Separate facts already available for use from the actual unresolved difference. Independent route formation does not require ignorance of known conclusions. A short derivation can make the search precise, but do not defer the first comparison until a long proof or several expansion rounds are complete. Routine algebra and unchanged applications do not trigger searches.

For explicitly offline-first work, fix permitted input and save our precise claim, route and falsifiable test. Name the comparison point in the existing PLAN before further multi-round expansion; defer retrieval to the next authorized connected boundary. If continued isolation is required, record the unresolved duplication risk without claiming novelty or silently changing mode. Main candidates still trigger immediate certification.

At selection, substantial candidates, strategy reviews and batch extensions, check whether the saved comparison answers the current decision. A checkpoint is not an instruction to browse. Unknown overlap permits provisional bounded exploration, not novelty claims or indefinite derivation-only extensions. Respect continuous commitments; this protocol adds no automatic hold or calls.

## Two questions with different reading depth

First check coverage: is the claim implied, contradicted, partly covered, or unresolved by the sources examined? Search at three useful scales: the original question, the substantial intermediate claim, and its standard/equivalent formulation. Use a survey or textbook to locate terminology and seed references when needed. From the closest source, follow relevant references backward and citations forward, including later versions/corrections; use author and MSC searches in MathSciNet/zbMATH where accessible, or scholarly search and author repositories. Record actual channels and inaccessible coverage, not a fictional exhaustive search. AI suggestions and search snippets are leads, not theorem evidence.

Read theorem statements and enough context to match hypotheses. Before rejecting a close result, test standard specialization, normalization, duality, reformulation or a short chain of implications. Different notation/definitions or failure of direct substitution is not evidence of novelty. Show the actual implication or the precise obstruction; do not assume every apparent mismatch is removable. Finish with: nearest result → exact difference → transfer tested → decision to reuse, verify, investigate the difference, or retrieve missing evidence. Inspect proofs when coverage or validity requires it; never hide contrary evidence.

Method learning has a separate purpose, not a mandatory later start. Before prolonged investment, inspect how the nearest work handles the same difficult step when that could change the route; do not wait for repeated failure. Read only the relevant mechanism, using cross-field.md for structural transfers. Write the object/operator dictionary, implication direction, extra hypotheses and an actual application test. Literature popularity does not warrant abandoning an independent route. Distinguish missing information, an unproved mathematical obligation and an obstacle introduced by our model.

## Reuse known results; reproduce for an explicit purpose

Once coverage and applicability are checked, record the known theorem with attribution and use it in the original dependency chain. Prove the new application/bridge and remaining difference, not the entire source theorem again by default. This is a literature-derived input, not a newly discovered local result; source status, applicability checks and local proof evidence remain separate. No automatic evidence-level promotion follows from publication or reuse.

Reproduction needs a purpose stated before the attempt in the existing PLAN/note: verify a doubtful step or check a convention/constant/endpoint or application. A concrete extension or alternative-proof objective must identify the new contribution and reuse the covered portions. Learning normally means reading the relevant proof, not reconstructing the whole known theorem. Name the portion to reconstruct and the stopping condition. If overlap is discovered after independent work, preserve the derivation, correct attribution, and stop rebuilding the covered part; do not retrospectively label it planned verification. Report reproduction/verification separately from new mathematical progress. A useful alternative proof may be pursued for its stated contribution, not merely because it was independently found.

## Reuse searches by mathematical scope

Use existing references.md or a round note, not a new database. Keep a compact reusable entry: target and assumptions; standard terms/equivalent formulation; date, actual queries/channels and relevant citation trails; author/title/year and stable link; version and theorem/page plus evidence location; what was read; coverage and hypothesis match/short reduction; usable input, remaining difference and next decision. Give recurring results a stable local reference/anchor. Mark known input, application, deliberate reproduction, potential difference or overlap unresolved separately from proof status. An empty search never establishes openness.

Distinguish a checked scope from incomplete retrieval. A failed URL or a nonapplicable nearby theorem does not clear the claim for novelty. If the missing comparison controls the next major investment, make its next distinct retrieval/transfer check explicit at the decision boundary; reuse other settled entries. For blocked access, try a meaningful alternative such as DOI, author manuscript or another version within scope, then retain the source GAP. Do not repeat the same failed request without a new reason or turn retrieval into an indefinite hunt.

Before browsing or declaring a source missing, locate existing material within the permitted input boundary: follow current comparison pointers, then use a targeted `rg` for author/title/DOI/reference ID and `rg --files` for likely local source names. Search the relevant project references/notes/source directories; consult shared/library records only if allowed. Do not read every historical log. An old failed URL does not mean the original text is unavailable locally; distinguish unavailable text from an untested application. Record the existing source path, version/theorem and exact next hypothesis check. Offline and isolated branches must not discover external material outside their allowlist.

Read only the relevant saved comparison and evidence slices. Search again only if a changed claim/hypothesis falls outside checked coverage, new contrary evidence or a source revision matters, a specific unsearched bridge appears, or a time-sensitive current-status/publication check is required. A new round, renamed task, routine lemma or batch boundary alone is not a trigger. Unchanged failed queries/access attempts are also reusable records: do not repeat them without a new source, access route or other reason. Their result remains unknown, not negative proof.

Batch closely related query variants; fetch and record decisive text once where permitted. Reuse checked text for unchanged applications, reading a new slice only for a new dependency or uncertainty. After locating a decisive source, finish the hypothesis/transfer check instead of collecting more equivalent hits. Do not routinely regenerate broad surveys or load whole papers/ledgers into every packet. Recheck original text whenever its meaning or provenance is uncertain; token savings never justify omitting assumptions or treating a summary as proof.

## Reconcile offline exploration with literature

At the planned boundary freeze the dated offline claims, assumptions, route, evidence pointers and actual obstacle in existing artifacts. Prepare precise comparison questions. In the next authorized connected step, consult the saved search record, fill only missing comparisons and test any proposed tool transfer. Preserve the original derivation and source labels; correct attribution when it is known. A known theorem may replace a rebuilt lemma, but its actual contribution to the original gap must be shown. Reproduction alone is not grounds for extra rounds; valid proofs are not downgraded merely because they are known.

Carry a concise, evidenced comparison into subsequent work. Work informed by external results is mixed; turning off the network does not restore blindness. A strictly isolated run needs an explicit permitted-input boundary, and a researcher who has read a paper cannot unsee it by hiding it from the summary. This sequence reduces fresh external steering, not training-data influence or all overlap risk.

If connected reconciliation is already authorized, perform it within scope and budget without asking again. Otherwise keep the handoff pending and obey the offline boundary. Do not silently switch a runner, add calls or interrupt a sealed round. In mixed-isolated, branches read only their own permitted inputs; the connected branch searches its frozen input, and integration compares frozen reports without new browsing. New search questions go to a subsequent authorized connected step. No extra agents or delegation are authorized here.

## Persist through existing writer rules

Manual work may update project references.md. V2 researchers save notes/<round>/references.md or the comparison in their proof note; include the needed hashed slice in ROUND_RESULT.evidence and its role/gap in summary or next_target. Retain the relevant inherited comparison pointer when later steps still need it; carry a short entry and direct pointers, not a growing bibliography. The writer preserves pointers; do not edit protected indexes or add required schema fields. Consult these pointers before trusting a stale top-level placeholder. Label sources internal-offline/provided-source/web-source/mixed accurately. Update routes/dependencies only for mathematical status changes.

A dedicated, authorized literature task can finish with an evidenced coverage decision or a checked reusable input; do not append an artificial lemma to make it count. A bibliography alone does not satisfy a proof/construction task, and a literature task does not silently replace a sealed mathematical acceptance. Use existing progress fields honestly: repeat for reconstruction without a new effect, enabling-result for an actual checked application, inconclusive when coverage remains unresolved. Full-main-problem coverage still invokes the applicable audit/pause; attribution and verification duties remain unchanged.
